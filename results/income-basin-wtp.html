Willingness to pay by income

Each cell is 10 yes/no draws at each of 10 posted bids, from $20 to $3,000. The model never names a dollar. The number is recovered from those votes.

Yes-share at a bid is the count of Yes votes divided by the number of parsed votes. Those ten shares are fit to P(yes) = 1 / (1 + exp(-(a + b x bid))). When the fitted slope b is negative, the reported willingness to pay is the price where that curve equals one half: -a / b. That price does not have to be one of the ten bids. If the shares are still mostly yes at $3,000 but drifting down, -a / b lands a few hundred dollars past the ladder. $3,108 and $3,295 are that kind of figure.

"Above $3,000" means the fit produced no median. Every parsed vote was yes, or the fitted slope was not negative, and the yes-share at $3,000 was still above one half. The estimate is then left censored at the top of the ladder. If a fit fails while the share at $3,000 is already at or below one half, the cell instead uses the Turnbull mean, the area under the yes-share curve from a price of zero to $3,000. That mean cannot exceed $3,000.

$75,000 watershed figures are the Saturday run. The basin column at $75,000 finished 28 September 2026. On the frozen rule (first bid where yes-share falls below half), the basin is at least the local watershed for Sonnet 5 and for Terra where both are identified. GPT-4o one-level fails: local stays above $3,000 and the basin crosses at $3,000. Sonnet 4.5, GPT-4o mini, and Haiku stay above the ladder in both columns, so those cells are unscored. Source: logs/income/35000, logs/income/200000, logs/income/75000, and logs/referendum. Advisor frame. Dollar figures are the logit median from src/wtp.py.
